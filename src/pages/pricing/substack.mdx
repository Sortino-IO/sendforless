<section class="verdict" id="answer">
  <p class="kicker"><span class="kicker-dot" aria-hidden="true"></span> Substack pricing</p>
  <h2>The platform fee is 10% of what readers pay, not a tier.</h2>
  <p>The source is <a href="https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037607131-How-much-does-Substack-cost">How much does Substack cost?</a>, updated 29 October 2026 and re-read 24 November 2026. Publishing stays free no matter how many subscribers you have. Readers pay the monthly, annual, or founding price you set. Substack does not add a fee on top of web payments beyond the 10% and Stripe. Tax, if you add it, is separate. Some European readers may see local payment methods, and those Stripe rates depend on the country of the Stripe account.</p>
</section>

These are platform fees only. They assume the revenue number is what readers paid for subscriptions. They do not include Stripe, refunds, or tax.

<p>A custom domain is $50 once. It is not a monthly line. In-app purchases on iOS may be priced higher so Apple's fee is covered, or left at the web price so you absorb it. You choose that in the settings the help article describes.</p>

## Stripe, stated as Substack states it

For a US card the article quotes 2.9% + $0.30 per payment, and 0.7% for Stripe Billing on recurring payments, noting the Billing figure as of July 2024. Local methods have their own rates. The article's US examples include iDEAL at 80 cents, Bancontact and Sofort at 1.4% + 30 cents, and SEPA Direct Debit at 0.8% + 30 cents, capped at $6, with a $10 charge on failed or disputed SEPA payments. Use the row that matches how your readers pay. Do not add all of them.

To see a real fee, the article says to open a succeeded payment in the Stripe dashboard and read the breakdown, then check payouts for the Billing line.
