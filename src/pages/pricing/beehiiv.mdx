## Free, Lite, Pro, and Enterprise

beehiiv sells four plans. The plan picks the features. The subscriber tier picks the price inside Lite and Pro. beehiiv asks you not to interpolate between rows: 3,200 subscribers is the up-to-5,000 price, not a point on a line between 2,500 and 5,000.

**Free** is $0. It includes up to 2,500 subscribers and unlimited sends, a website, a podcast within 5,000 downloads a month, the recommendation network, and a read-only API and MCP. It does not include automations, paid subscriptions, the ad network, A/B tests, or custom domains. One publication, one seat, analytics lookback of 30 days, three segments. It is a real product for a new letter. It is not a trial of Lite with the logo left on.

**Lite** is the plan most growing newsletters actually buy. It adds paid subscriptions at a 0% platform fee, the ad network (not in every region, and not during the trial), automations, polls, A/B tests, custom domains, surveys, and write access to the API and MCP. Limits from beehiiv’s feature table: up to 100,000 subscribers, 2 seats, 3 guest authors, 3 publications, 12 months of analytics, 10 custom pages, 10 live automations, 10 segments. Branding stays. If removing “powered by beehiiv” matters, that is Pro, not a setting.

**Pro** adds branding removal, up to 250,000 subscribers, 10 seats, 10 publications, unlimited guest authors, unlimited analytics history, dynamic content, webhooks without the Lite cap of three, click-triggered automations, and priority support. It is the plan beehiiv marks as the one for a content business rather than a solo letter. At every bucket we checked, Pro is $50 to $230 a month more than Lite on monthly billing. That gap is the branding, the team, and the ceiling. It is not a second copy of the send button.

**Enterprise** is a conversation. Custom subscriber limits, SSO, dedicated IPs, a success manager. There is no public price, so we will not invent one.

beehiiv’s October 2026 post maps the old names: Launch became Free, Scale became Lite, Max became Pro. New customers are on the new grid. Existing customers switch at the first renewal on or after 1 November 2026. Annual subscribers keep their current rate until the term renews. If you are comparing a friend’s invoice to this page, ask whether they have renewed.

A few buckets people ask for that are not their own row:

- **7,500** pays the 10,000 price. Lite $139 monthly.
- **15,000** pays the 25,000 price. Lite $199 monthly. The jump from 10,000 to 25,000 is a wide step. Crossing 10,001 is a $60 monthly increase on Lite, not a gentle slope.

Free still covers 1,000 and 2,500 if you do not need Lite’s features. The table is the price of needing them, or of simply having more subscribers than 2,500.

## Yearly versus monthly

beehiiv says yearly billing saves up to about 20% against month-to-month. The table matches that shape. At 1,000 subscribers, Lite monthly is $59 and the annual effective rate is $49, which is $588 a year instead of $708. You save $120 and you prepay. At 10,000, Lite monthly is $139 and annual is $1,428 instead of $1,668. The saving is $240 and the check you write in January is the whole $1,428.

Annual is the right default if you already know you will use the product for the year. It is the wrong default if you are testing whether recommendations or the ad network work for your topic. A monthly Lite bill is the option value. MailCheap’s yearly rule is different: you pay for 10 months and get 12, which is a larger percentage off a much smaller base. Ten times $19 is $190. Do not compare “20% off” with “two months free” without comparing the base. A percentage off an expensive plan can still be the expensive plan.

Taxes, local currency, and any private arrangement are not in this table. The pricing page is USD list price. Your card statement can differ.

Over a year of monthly billing, a 4,000-person weekly letter is $1,308 on beehiiv Lite, $228 on MailCheap Starter, $900 on Ghost(Pro) Publisher, and $0 on Kit Free or Substack if you never enable the paid features those $0 plans withhold. That spread is the reason this site exists. It is not a ranking of quality. Kit Free will not run beehiiv’s ad network. Substack will take 10% the moment you charge. Ghost’s $75 includes memberships and a real site. MailCheap’s $19 includes the send and excludes the marketplace. Pick the row whose exclusions you can live with.

MailerLite belongs in the same conversation and is easy to forget because it is not a “creator” brand. Comfort at the 5,000-subscriber tier, which contains 4,000 people, is $49 a month and allows 50,000 emails, enough for the weekly issue. At 10,000 it is $89. At 25,000 it is $179. Power, with unlimited emails, is $69, $129, and $239 at those tiers. The full grid is on the [MailerLite alternatives](/alternatives/mailerlite/) page, from MailerLite’s own pricing markdown.

Add the costs that never appear as the platform fee. A custom domain is a yearly registrar bill, except where a host includes the first year. Stripe takes its cut on every paid tool except the ones that have not turned payments on. Your time to rebuild automations is not zero. A deliverability consultant is optional and not cheap. “What a newsletter costs” is the platform plus the payment processor plus the hour you spend on DNS. The platform is the only line we can put in a table without pretending the others are universal.

## Unlimited sends, and the catch

beehiiv is explicit that no plan meters email volume. A daily briefing to 10,000 people is still the $139 Lite tier, not a surprise overage. That is the feature to value if your cadence is bursty. It is wasted money if you send once a week and you are only on Lite because you crossed 2,500 names.

The catch is the feature gate, not the send gate. Recommendations exist on Free. The referral program, paid subscriptions, ads, automations, and A/B tests do not. Writers sometimes upgrade at 800 subscribers because they want the paywall, then feel the tier climb later and blame the send. The send was never the expensive part. The bucket was.

Podcast downloads, community notifications, and digital-product counts have their own caps in the feature table. “Unlimited email” is not “unlimited everything.” A podcast that crosses 5,000 downloads a month on Free, or 15,000 on Lite, is a plan change even if the email list is calm. Read the row for the product you actually use.

## When Lite is worth the invoice

Pay for Lite if you will charge readers, sell through the ad network, or run automations you do not already have somewhere else. The 0% take rate is the argument against Substack, and it is a good argument once subscription revenue is larger than the Lite bill. At $109 a month, you need more than $1,090 a month of subscription revenue before beehiiv’s 0% beats Substack’s 10% on the revenue alone, and that comparison ignores Substack’s discovery and beehiiv’s other tools. Do the math with your price and your conversion, not with a slogan. Our [beehiiv vs Substack](/compare/beehiiv-vs-substack/) piece walks through it.

Do not pay for Lite if you are under 2,500 and Free already sends the issue. Do not pay for Pro to get features you have not used on Lite, unless the specific Pro item — branding removal, a tenth seat, dynamic content — is the reason. Do not pay either tier if the only job is “mail this list every Sunday” and a $19 or $0 tool will do it. The alternatives roundup is [beehiiv alternatives](/alternatives/beehiiv/). Moving the list is a CSV export, documented in the [MailCheap comparison](/compare/mailcheap-vs-beehiiv/) if that is the destination, and it is the wrong destination if you still need the paywall.

The pricing page can change. When it does, the markdown file beehiiv publishes next to it is the artifact to re-read, not a blog post from last spring. We will update this guide when we re-read it. Until then, the table above is the 24 November 2026 list price, and your checkout is the price that matters.
