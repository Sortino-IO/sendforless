Mailchimp is a marketing suite that can send a newsletter. It fits a shop that already lives there. It is an awkward fit for a one-person letter that only needs a send. The limits below are from Mailchimp's help center and marketing pricing page, checked 25 November 2026.

## What is Mailchimp and what is it used for?

Mailchimp is Intuit's email marketing software for storing contacts and sending campaigns. Mailchimp is used for email campaigns, forms, landing pages, automations, and a contact database that can connect to a store. A typical account collects an audience, builds a campaign in a template or the drag-and-drop builder, and sends it on a schedule if the plan includes scheduling. Free Mailchimp accounts can send, inside 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, with a daily cap of 250. A paid plan is what adds scheduling, longer automations, and logo removal.

Mailchimp's cost is a plan price plus a contact tier. Essentials starts at $13 a month and Standard at $20 a month, both for up to 500 contacts. Premium starts at $350 a month, and the help article says that base includes 10,000 contacts. Above 500 contacts on Essentials and Standard, check current pricing on the marketing pricing page. The full price table is on [Mailchimp pricing](/pricing/mailchimp/).

## What Mailchimp features come with each plan?

Mailchimp features split by plan. The marketing pricing page and About Mailchimp Pricing Plans, both checked 25 November 2026, are the sources.

**Builder and templates.** Free includes a limited template set. Essentials includes all Mailchimp email templates. Standard and Premium can create and send custom-coded templates. Free and Essentials can create a custom-coded template. Sending it requires the higher plan. Scheduling is on the paid plans, not on Free.

**Marketing Automation Flows.** Free does not include them. Essentials includes flows and templates of up to 4 steps. The pricing page describes Standard and Premium flows as up to 200 steps, with branching. A welcome series longer than 4 steps is a Standard feature, not an Essentials feature.

**Segmentation.** Tags and basic segments are how you stop mailing everyone. Advanced segmentation is a Standard feature in the help article. Predictive segments are listed for Standard and Premium, not for Free or Essentials. If the reason you pay is a predicted group of buyers, you are on Standard or above, and the contact tier is still separate from that feature.

**Forms and landing pages.** Popup forms are on the plan table for every marketing plan, including Free. The help article says Free can try landing pages and forms. They are not a separate product you have to buy before the first signup form.

**Website.** Mailchimp sells a Websites plan alongside marketing. Pausing the marketing plan does not, by itself, cancel a Websites plan. The help article on pausing says the Websites plan stays active. If you only wanted email, check that you are not also paying for the site.

**SMS.** SMS is an add-on on paid plans in select countries, after an application. MMS is on Standard and Premium in select locations. Credits are bought in blocks, expire monthly, and do not roll over. The credit price is on Mailchimp's SMS pages. It is not in the $13 or $20 start.

**AI.** Generative AI is included at no extra cost on Standard and Premium, in the countries and languages Mailchimp lists. It is not on Free or Essentials.

**Integrations.** The pricing page lists 300+ integrations on every plan, including Free. A connected store is the integration that makes leaving expensive, because purchase history and journeys do not ride along in a contact CSV.

## What support does each Mailchimp plan include?

Free includes email support for the first 30 days. Essentials and Standard include 24/7 email and chat. Premium includes phone support and priority email and chat, plus a longer onboarding (the pricing page lists four sessions on Premium and one on Standard). Phone support is English. Personalized onboarding is for new or upgraded Standard and Premium accounts in the first 90 days, in the languages the pricing page names.

## How does Mailchimp say to authenticate a domain?

Mailchimp's help article Set Up Email Domain Authentication (checked 25 November 2026) describes the setup. You verify the sending address first, then authenticate the domain you own. Automatic setup uses Entri to write the DNS records. Manual setup is two CNAME records for DKIM and one TXT record for DMARC. Public addresses such as a free Gmail or Yahoo mailbox cannot be authenticated. Gmail and Yahoo require custom authentication, and they require a published DMARC record if you send more than 5,000 emails to those addresses in 24 hours. Validation can take up to 48 hours. If you later change the CNAME records, Mailchimp says to turn authentication off and set it up again.

## What do public Mailchimp reviews say?

Reviews on G2's Intuit Mailchimp Email Marketing page show 4.3 out of 5 from 13,001 reviews. G2 lists that page as last updated 1 November 2026. That figure is G2's aggregate, not a score from sendforless. G2's summary of those reviews praises templates and the drag-and-drop builder, and it notes that price can rise as the contact list grows. Read the current G2 page if you want the live count. The complaints that match the plan rules above are the contact bill and the jump from a 4-step automation to a longer one.

## What is a Mailchimp email campaign?

A Mailchimp email campaign is one message to the people you select. The builder is where that message is written. Templates cover the common layouts. A custom HTML template can be drafted on Free and sent on Standard or Premium. Each recipient counts as one send. A campaign to 500 people is 500 sends, and a test to yourself counts too. On Free, 500 sends is the whole month, with a daily cap of 250, so a weekly letter to 200 people (about 870 sends) does not fit even though the headcount does. On a paid plan the monthly allowance is 10, 12, or 15 times the contact limit, depending on Essentials, Standard, or Premium. Schedule the campaign only on a plan that includes scheduling. A/B testing of a single email starts on Essentials. Multivariate testing is a Standard feature in the help article. If the stored contacts include people who unsubscribed, they still count toward the tier until you archive them, even when the campaign itself goes only to subscribed people.

Seats and audiences follow the same plan split. Free is 1 seat and 1 audience. Essentials is 3 and 3. Standard is 5 and 5. Premium is unlimited audiences and unlimited seats, with Owner, Admin, Author, Manager, and Viewer roles. Essentials seats are Owner and Admin. If a second person only needs to read reports, Standard is the first plan with a Viewer role.

Pay As You Go is the other way to pay. It uses the Essentials feature set and sells email credits instead of a monthly contact tier. The help article describes it for people who send a few times a year. The credit price is on the pricing page. A nonprofit can request 15% off a marketing plan before buying it. The discount is not retroactive. United States groups add an EIN first. That request is a billing form, documented on About the Nonprofit Discount, checked 25 November 2026.

## Who does Mailchimp fit?

Mailchimp fits a business that keeps customers in audiences, sends more than a newsletter, and may connect a store. Essentials, from $13 a month at up to 500 contacts, adds a schedule, templates, and automations of up to 4 steps. Standard, from $20 a month at the same 500 contacts, lengthens those flows and adds more seats. Premium, from $350 a month, is the plan with phone support, and the help article says the base includes 10,000 contacts. The full ladder is [Mailchimp pricing](/pricing/mailchimp/). Do not treat those starts as the price of a 5,000-contact account.

It also fits a brand-new list that is still under 250 contacts and 500 sends a month. The free plan is real. A weekly letter can outgrow the send cap before it outgrows the contact cap. About 870 sends a month is a weekly letter to 200 people, which is over 500.

It fits poorly when you are paying to store people who unsubscribed. Subscribed, unsubscribed, and non-subscribed contacts all count. Archived, cleaned, and deleted contacts do not. Archive first, then decide whether the suite is still the right home.

## Why do people leave Mailchimp?

People leave because the contact total and the list they mail are different numbers. A year of unsubscribes still sits on the bill. They leave because the tier above 500 contacts is a slider, not a page you can compare in a spreadsheet, so the renewal feels opaque next to MailerLite's published ladder or Flodesk's flat $46. They leave because a newsletter does not use the journeys, and a cheaper send or a free broadcast plan does the one job.

They leave a shop less often. Journeys, purchase data, and ad audiences do not come along in the contact CSV. Rebuilding that on a sender with no store takes the most work. If that rebuild is the fear, stay and price the tier you can see in the account. The [Mailchimp alternatives](/alternatives/mailchimp/) page is for the case where the store is not the reason.

Export and cancel steps are on [Mailchimp alternatives](/alternatives/mailchimp/).

## What are the downsides?

The downsides are structural. You pay for contacts, not for the people you mailed this month. The free plan's send cap is 500 a month and 250 a day, which is tight for a weekly habit. Automations on Essentials stop at 4 steps, so a longer welcome series needs Standard. Phone support waits for Premium. Tests and transactional mail count toward the send limit. Going over a paid contact limit adds a charge instead of stopping the month, which protects delivery and surprises the invoice.

A second downside is comparison. At 4,000 contacts, beehiiv Lite is $109, MailerLite Comfort is $49, Flodesk Email is $46, and MailCheap Starter is $19 if the weekly emails fit. Mailchimp at 4,000 contacts is check current pricing. You cannot honestly rank it until you read your tier. That opacity is a downside even when the tier turns out to be fair.

## Is Mailchimp a CRM, or a newsletter tool?

Is Mailchimp a CRM? It keeps a contact record: tags, audiences, and, with a connected store, purchases. You can segment and mail those people. It does not replace a pipeline CRM with deals, stages, and a forecast. Calling it a CRM is fair for a small shop's customer list and unfair for a sales team.

For a newsletter, Mailchimp works within the caps above. The editor, templates, and scheduling are on the paid plans. Standard adds custom-coded templates you can send, and generative AI at no extra cost in the countries Mailchimp lists. Free can create a custom-coded template and cannot send it. If the newsletter is the whole company, a publication tool (beehiiv, Substack, Ghost) or a creator sender (Kit) matches the job more closely than a suite built around audiences.

## When is Mailchimp worth it, and what are the pros and cons?

Pros: a free plan that really sends, published starts at $13 and $20 for 500 contacts, automations, a large template set on paid plans, and a store that can stay connected. The nonprofit discount is 15% after approval, on the help page, and it is not retroactive. Pay As You Go exists for people who mail a few times a year, with the Essentials feature set. The credit price is on Mailchimp's page.

Cons: unsubscribed contacts count, the free send cap is low, prices above 500 contacts are not a public table, and the suite is more product than a letter needs. Journeys do not move when you leave.

Mailchimp is worth it for the shop and the journeys, once the tier is a number you have seen. It is worth it under the free caps. It is not worth a guessed "it gets expensive" if you have not archived inactive contacts and read the new tier. It is not worth it for a broadcast-only list under 10,000 subscribers when Kit Free is $0, unless you are about to need sequences. Creator, for those sequences, is $39 a month up to 1,000 subscribers.
