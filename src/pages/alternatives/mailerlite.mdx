MailerLite alternatives in 2026 start from a rename, not from a new product. On 16 June 2026 Growing Business became Comfort and Advanced became Power. The pricing file at mailerlite.com/pricing.md, last updated 8 September 2026 and checked on 25 November 2026, is the table. HTML of the same page is at mailerlite.com/pricing. If you use the sites, the automations, and the paid newsletter, the bill can still be the one that fits. If you pay Comfort only to broadcast, a free plan or a sender that meters emails is the comparison.

## When should you stay with MailerLite?

Stay when the extra tools are in use. Comfort includes unlimited newsletter templates, 50 automations, 10 websites and landing pages, 5 digital products or bookings, multivariate testing, logo removal, a preference center, and an AI writing assistant. Power removes the caps on automations, sites, forms, and products, adds multiple automation triggers, and includes 24/7 live chat. Free is a real account, not a preview: 250 subscribers, 2,500 emails, 3 automations, one website, one landing page, and three forms.

Stay under 250 subscribers if Free still sends. Buying Comfort to escape a $0 plan that fits is the expensive version of the same letter.

Stay if you looked at Mailchimp to save money and have not read the Mailchimp tier. Essentials starts at $13 a month and Standard at $20, both for up to 500 contacts. Above 500 contacts, Mailchimp's price is on its pricing page. The head-to-head is [MailerLite vs Mailchimp](/compare/mailerlite-vs-mailchimp/). The decision to remain is also the [MailerLite review](/reviews/mailerlite/).

## What do MailerLite alternatives cost against Comfort and Power?

MailerLite prices by active subscribers. Unsubscribed and bounced addresses do not count. An address that was active at any point in the billing cycle still counts until the anniversary, even if you delete it sooner. Comfort's email allowance is 10 times the ceiling of the tier, not 10 times your exact count. Power's emails are unlimited up to 200,000 subscribers, subject to fair use. Annual billing is 10% off the monthly rate. Enterprise is custom above 200,000.

## Which MailerLite alternatives are free or close to it?

**Kit (ConvertKit)** is the free broadcast plan. It is $0 up to 10,000 subscribers, with unlimited broadcasts, forms, landing pages, tagging, and segmentation. Sequences and visual automations are paid. Creator is $39 a month up to 1,000 subscribers and $59 a month up to 3,000 in the billing help. Pro is $79 a month at 1,000 subscribers. If the MailerLite account is a weekly letter and nothing else, Kit Free is the MailerLite alternative that changes the invoice to zero until 10,000. You give up the 50 automations and the 10 sites. [Kit pricing](/pricing/convertkit/).

**beehiiv Free** is $0 up to 2,500 subscribers and unlimited sends, including the recommendation network. It does not include automations or paid subscriptions. Lite at 4,000 subscribers is $109 a month, which is more than Comfort at $49. Choose beehiiv when recommendations, ads, or a 0% cut of paid subscriptions are the product. Choose Comfort when they are not. [beehiiv pricing](/pricing/beehiiv/).

**Substack** is $0 to publish at any size. Readers who pay trigger a 10% fee plus Stripe. A custom domain is $50 once. It does not replace Comfort's automation cap or a stack of landing pages. It replaces a monthly tier when the publication can live on Substack's editor and the 10% is still smaller than Comfort. [Substack pricing](/pricing/substack/).

**MailerLite Free** is still the right $0 plan under 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails. Switching away from Free to another free plan is optional, not a savings.

## Which paid MailerLite alternatives change the bill?

**MailCheap** charges for emails, with a subscriber ceiling. Starter is $19 a month for 20,000 emails and 10,000 subscribers. Growth is $39 for 75,000 emails and 40,000 subscribers. Pro is $79 for 200,000 emails and 100,000 subscribers. A weekly letter to 10,000 people is about 43,300 emails, which is Growth at $39 against Comfort at $89. A weekly letter to 25,000 people is about 108,300 emails, which is Pro at $79 against Comfort at $179. MailCheap does not sell paid newsletters, an ad network, a referral program, or A/B tests. There is no hosted free plan. Yearly billing costs 10 months. Use it when the cadence fits and the missing tools are acceptable. The [MailCheap review](/reviews/mailcheap/) is the limit list. Links to MailCheap are affiliate links.

**Flodesk Email** is $46 a month at any list size, or $38 a month billed annually ($456 a year). At 1,000 subscribers Comfort is $19, so Flodesk is the higher bill. At 5,000, Comfort is $49 and Flodesk is $46, close enough that the templates matter more than the dollar. Everything, which adds checkout, is $99 a month or $79 annually. The free plan does not send.

**Ghost(Pro)** is the membership site. Publisher is $35 a month at 1,000 members, $75 up to 5,000, and $105 up to 10,000. Starter is $18 up to 1,000 members and cannot charge readers. There is no hosted free plan. The platform fee is 0%. Pick Ghost when members log in. Pick Comfort when a site of up to 10 pages and a newsletter are enough.

**Mailchimp** is a sideways move until you read the tier. At 500 contacts Essentials is $13 and Comfort is $12. Above 500 contacts, check current pricing. Mailchimp counts unsubscribed people. MailerLite does not. A store that already runs in Mailchimp is a reason to accept the higher tier. A newsletter that does not is not.

## What do MailerLite alternatives leave out?

Every cheaper option drops something Comfort includes.

Kit Free drops sequences, visual automations, and paid recommendations. You can look at those screens. Creating one asks you to upgrade. Digital products on Free carry a fee of 3.5% plus 30 cents per sale, card processing included, on Kit's pricing page.

beehiiv Free drops automations, the ad network, and paid subscriptions. Lite adds them and charges the subscriber bucket. Sends stay unlimited, which is the opposite of Comfort's cap.

Substack drops a private automation builder of Comfort's size and takes 10% when readers pay. You do not get Comfort's multivariate tests or a stack of sites you theme like Ghost.

MailCheap drops paid newsletters, multivariate tests, and a website builder. It counts every email, including tests and welcomes. Comfort counts emails only up to the cap, then stops unless you move to Power.

Ghost drops the "email plus a few pages" shape. You are running a site. Starter cannot charge members. Publisher can, at a higher monthly number than Comfort until the list is large.

Flodesk drops a subscriber ladder and, on the Email plan, checkout. You pay $46 even at 300 people.

## How does the email cap change which MailerLite alternative fits?

The cap is why two newsletters with the same subscriber count get different bills, and why some MailerLite alternatives only win on a daily letter.

At 1,000 subscribers, one weekly issue is about 4,300 emails. Comfort allows 10,000. You have room for a welcome message and a test. Power at $39 is optional. Kit Free is $0 if you can skip sequences. MailCheap Starter at $19 also fits, and it is more than $0.

At 2,500 subscribers, a weekly issue is about 10,800 emails. Comfort's up-to-2,500 tier allows 25,000 emails and costs $33. A daily issue is about 75,800 emails and does not fit. Power at that tier is $49. beehiiv Free still covers 2,500 people if you do not need automations. The moment you do, Lite at the up-to-2,500 bucket is $79 a month, above both Comfort and Power.

At 8,000 subscribers, Comfort is $69 and allows 80,000 emails. A weekly issue is about 34,600 emails, so Comfort still fits. Growth on MailCheap is $39 for 75,000 emails and 40,000 subscribers, which also fits a weekly letter and costs less, without Comfort's sites. A second weekly issue is about 69,300 emails. Comfort still covers it. MailCheap Growth still covers it. Add welcomes and the MailCheap plan can step to Pro at $79, while Comfort stays $69 until the 80,000 emails are gone.

At 20,000 subscribers, Comfort is $159 and allows 200,000 emails. A weekly issue is about 86,600 emails. MailCheap Pro is $79 for 200,000 emails. Comfort is the higher bill unless the sites and the 50 automations are in use. Power at that tier is $219. You would buy Power for a daily letter, about 606,000 emails, or for unlimited automations, not to save money against Pro.

These MailerLite alternatives are not interchangeable at the cap. Kit does not meter sends on any plan. beehiiv does not meter sends. Ghost does not meter sends. Flodesk does not meter sends on paid plans. MailCheap and MailerLite Comfort do. If the calendar is one issue a week, the cap is rarely the reason to leave Comfort before the subscriber price is. If the calendar is daily, the cap is the whole decision, and Power, Kit, beehiiv, or MailCheap's next plan are the comparisons. Check Kit's price above 10,000 subscribers on Kit's page before you treat Free as permanent.

## What do you rebuild when you leave?

MailerLite's migration page is their guide for arriving, not for leaving. Leaving is an export of subscribers, then a rebuild. Forms, automations, and sites do not become a Kit sequence or a MailCheap campaign by themselves. Tags can be columns in a CSV if the export includes them. Paid subscription state needs a tool that can bill it. MailCheap cannot. Ghost and beehiiv Lite can. Substack can, and then takes 10%.

Keep the MailerLite account until one campaign has gone out on the new tool and a reply to the old from-address still reaches you. Downgrades on MailerLite run to the end of the billing cycle. Switching from annual to monthly also waits until the annual term ends. Do not delete the audience on the day you export.

## Which MailerLite alternative fits a weekly letter, and which fits a daily one?

MailerLite alternatives for a weekly letter under 10,000 subscribers start with Kit (ConvertKit) Free at $0, if sequences can wait. MailerLite alternatives for the same letter once you want 50 automations and a handful of sites start with staying on Comfort, because the email cap usually still fits and the tools are the reason for the bill. MailerLite alternatives for a weekly letter past 10,000 subscribers, with no paywall, start with MailCheap when the email total fits Growth or Pro. At 10,000 weekly subscribers that is about 43,300 emails and Growth at $39, against Comfort at $89. At 25,000 it is about 108,300 emails and Pro at $79, against Comfort at $179.

A daily letter changes the short list. Comfort's cap is the constraint, not the feature list. At 5,000 subscribers a daily issue is about 151,700 emails. Comfort at that tier allows 50,000, so you are on Power at $69, or on a tool that does not meter: Kit, beehiiv, Ghost, or Flodesk at $46. MailCheap would need the plan whose email allowance covers about 151,700 plus welcomes. Pro allows 200,000 emails at $79. That can fit a daily letter at 5,000 and still cost less than beehiiv Lite, which is $109 at the up-to-5,000 bucket and does not meter. It costs more than Flodesk. Pick Power if you want to keep the MailerLite sites. Pick Flodesk if you want one price and the templates. Pick MailCheap if you want the lower counted send and you can live without the site builder.

MailerLite alternatives for a paid newsletter are fewer. Substack takes 10% plus Stripe and charges $0 until someone pays. beehiiv Lite takes 0% plus Stripe and charges the bucket. Ghost Publisher takes 0% plus Stripe and charges by members, $75 a month up to 5,000. Comfort charges the subscriber tier and can sell a recurring subscription. If revenue is small, 10% can be less than Comfort. If the site is the product, Ghost can be worth the higher monthly number. Do not leave Comfort for a tool that cannot bill readers and then rebuild payments somewhere else unless that was the plan.

The same MailerLite alternatives fail when the account is a store that already runs in Mailchimp. Journeys do not export. Read the Mailchimp tier, including the unsubscribed contacts, before you call Comfort or a MailCheap plan a savings. Above 500 Mailchimp contacts, that tier is check current pricing.

MailerLite alternatives that win on a weekly broadcast under 10,000 subscribers are Kit Free first. MailerLite alternatives that win on a counted weekly send past that, without a paywall, are MailCheap when the email total fits a published plan. MailerLite alternatives that cost more and buy a publication are beehiiv and Ghost. Stay on Comfort when the 50 automations, the sites, or the paid newsletter are why you have the account. The email cap is the reason to look at Power, not the reason to leave, if you are already using the rest.
