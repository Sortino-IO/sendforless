## Which MailerLite alternative fits you?

The right MailerLite alternative depends on what you send and what you sell. Here is the short version:

- **A weekly newsletter under 10,000 subscribers, no automations needed:** [Kit's free plan](/pricing/convertkit/) costs $0.
- **A free plan with unlimited sends, up to 2,500 subscribers:** [beehiiv Free](/pricing/beehiiv/).
- **A paid newsletter with no monthly fee:** [Substack](/pricing/substack/), which takes 10% of what readers pay.
- **A membership site where readers log in and pay:** [Ghost(Pro) Publisher](/pricing/ghost/).
- **A large list on a regular schedule, with no paywall:** [MailCheap](/reviews/mailcheap/), which charges by emails sent.
- **One flat price at any list size:** [Flodesk Email](/pricing/flodesk/) at $46 a month.
- **You use MailerLite's automations, websites or paid newsletter tools:** stay on MailerLite and compare Comfort with Power.

Prices on this page are monthly list prices in US dollars from each company's pricing page, as of November 25, 2026. Annual prices are listed separately.

## Why people look for MailerLite alternatives

**The price rises with every subscriber tier.** Comfort costs $12 a month at 500 subscribers, $49 at 5,000, $89 at 10,000 and $179 at 25,000. MailerLite bills by active subscribers, so unsubscribed and bounced addresses don't count. Any address that was active during a billing cycle still counts until your next billing date, even if you delete it.

**Comfort has a monthly email limit.** You get 10 times the top of your subscriber tier. On the up-to-5,000 tier, that's 50,000 emails a month. A weekly newsletter to 5,000 people uses about 21,700 emails, so it fits. A daily one uses about 151,700, so it doesn't. When you hit the limit, you can upgrade to Power or wait for the next billing cycle.

**The free plan is small.** MailerLite Free allows 250 active subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, with 3 automations, 1 website, 1 landing page and 3 signup forms. Once you pass 250 active subscribers, campaigns and automations stop sending until you upgrade or remove subscribers.

If you have seen the plan names Growing Business and Advanced, those are the old names. MailerLite renamed them Comfort and Power on June 16, 2026, and changed prices and limits at the same time.

## When staying on MailerLite makes sense

Stay if you use the tools you are paying for. Comfort includes 50 automations, 10 websites and landing pages, 5 digital products or bookings, 10 signup forms, paid newsletters, multivariate testing, a subscriber preference center and an AI writing assistant. Most cheaper MailerLite alternatives drop several of these, and rebuilding them elsewhere takes time.

Stay on Free if you have fewer than 250 subscribers. Paying for another tool makes little sense while MailerLite's free plan still covers your list.

If your only problem is the email limit, look at Power before you leave. Power removes the limits on emails, automations, websites, forms and products, adds multiple automation triggers and 24/7 live chat. At 5,000 subscribers it costs $69 a month, $20 more than Comfort.

If you are thinking about Mailchimp to save money, compare the numbers first in [MailerLite vs Mailchimp](/compare/mailerlite-vs-mailchimp/). For a full look at the product, read the [MailerLite review](/reviews/mailerlite/).

The cheapest of these MailerLite alternatives isn't always the right one. Kit Free has no automations, Substack takes a cut once readers pay, and beehiiv and Ghost cost more because they include publishing and monetization tools.

## Free MailerLite alternatives

### Kit (formerly ConvertKit)

Kit's free plan suits a newsletter that only needs broadcasts. It costs $0 up to 10,000 subscribers and includes unlimited broadcasts, forms and landing pages, tagging and segmentation, and recommendations through Kit's creator network. You can also sell digital products and subscriptions on Free, for 3.5% plus 30 cents per sale, card processing included.

The trade-off is automation. Email sequences and visual automations need the Creator plan, which costs $39 a month at 1,000 subscribers, $59 at 3,000, $89 at 5,000 and $139 at 10,000. Pro starts at $79 a month at 1,000 subscribers. Kit offers a free migration service on paid plans. See [Kit pricing](/pricing/convertkit/) for more tiers.

### beehiiv Free

beehiiv Free covers up to 2,500 subscribers with unlimited sends and access to beehiiv's recommendation network. It doesn't include automations, paid subscriptions or the ad network. Those start on Lite, which costs $59 a month at 1,000 subscribers, $79 at 2,500 and $109 at 5,000, or $49, $69 and $95 a month billed annually.

beehiiv makes sense if you plan to earn from ads or paid subscriptions, since Lite takes 0% of subscription revenue. If you don't need those, Lite costs more than Comfort at every list size up to 25,000 subscribers. See [beehiiv pricing](/pricing/beehiiv/).

### Substack

Substack is free to publish, no matter how many subscribers you have. If you turn on paid subscriptions, Substack keeps 10% of that revenue, and Stripe charges its own fees (2.9% plus 30 cents per US card payment, plus 0.7% for recurring billing). A custom domain is a one-time $50.

Substack suits writers who want to start a paid newsletter without a monthly bill. It doesn't replace MailerLite's automations or landing pages, and as revenue grows, 10% can cost more than a monthly plan. See [Substack pricing](/pricing/substack/).

### MailerLite Free

If you have fewer than 250 subscribers, MailerLite's own free plan is still a solid option. Moving to another free plan only helps if you need more subscribers or more sends than it allows.

## Paid MailerLite alternatives that change what you pay

### MailCheap

MailCheap charges for the emails you send, and each plan has a subscriber limit. Starter is $19 a month for 20,000 emails and up to 10,000 subscribers. Growth is $39 for 75,000 emails and 40,000 subscribers. Pro is $79 for 200,000 emails and 100,000 subscribers. Yearly billing costs the price of 10 months. Every email counts, including test, welcome and confirmation emails.

This model suits a large list that sends on a predictable schedule. A weekly newsletter to 10,000 subscribers uses about 43,300 emails, which fits Growth at $39, compared with $89 for Comfort. At 25,000 subscribers, it's Pro at $79 against $179.

What you give up: MailCheap doesn't sell paid subscriptions and has no ad network, referral program or A/B testing. There is no free plan or free trial. The [MailCheap review](/reviews/mailcheap/) covers the limits in detail.

### Flodesk

Flodesk charges one price at any list size. The Email plan is $46 a month, or $38 a month billed annually ($456 a year), with unlimited subscribers, unlimited emails and workflow automations. The Everything plan adds checkouts, sales pages and paid subscriptions for $99 a month, or $79 a month billed annually. Flodesk's free plan can't send email.

At 1,000 subscribers, Comfort is $19, so Flodesk costs more. At 5,000, Comfort is $49 and Flodesk is $46, so choose based on templates and workflow rather than price. Flodesk pulls ahead on cost for larger lists. See [Flodesk pricing](/pricing/flodesk/).

### Ghost(Pro)

Ghost suits publishers who want a full membership site, where readers sign in and pay. Publisher costs $35 a month for up to 1,000 members, $75 for up to 5,000 and $105 for up to 10,000. Ghost takes no cut of subscription payments. Starter is $18 a month for up to 1,000 members but doesn't include paid subscriptions. There is no free hosted plan.

Choose Ghost when the website is the product. If you only need a newsletter and a few landing pages, Comfort costs less. See [Ghost pricing](/pricing/ghost/).

### Mailchimp

Mailchimp isn't cheaper at the entry level. At 500 contacts, Essentials starts at $13 a month and Standard at $20, against $12 for Comfort. Mailchimp also counts unsubscribed contacts toward your bill, and MailerLite doesn't. Above 500 contacts, check current pricing on Mailchimp's site. The full comparison is in [MailerLite vs Mailchimp](/compare/mailerlite-vs-mailchimp/).

## Sending daily? Check the email limit first

How often you send matters as much as list size. Two newsletters with the same number of subscribers can need different plans.

At 5,000 subscribers, a daily newsletter uses about 151,700 emails a month. Comfort allows 50,000 at that tier, so on MailerLite you would need Power at $69. Here is how MailerLite alternatives handle the same schedule:

- **Kit:** no limit on broadcasts. Free costs $0 if you can skip automations, and Creator is $89 at 5,000 subscribers.
- **beehiiv:** unlimited sends. Lite costs $109 at 5,000 subscribers.
- **Flodesk:** unlimited emails for $46 a month.
- **Ghost(Pro):** priced by members, not emails. Publisher costs $75 at 5,000 members.
- **MailCheap:** Pro at $79 covers 200,000 emails, which leaves room for welcome and test emails.

For a weekly newsletter, the email limit rarely forces a change. At 1,000 subscribers, one issue a week uses about 4,300 of Comfort's 10,000 emails. At 20,000 subscribers, it uses about 86,700 of 200,000. In both cases, the subscriber price is the more likely reason to switch.

## What to plan for when you switch from MailerLite

Your subscribers move with a CSV export, which most MailerLite alternatives can import. Most other setup does not. Forms, automations and websites have to be rebuilt in the new tool, and tags only carry over if they are included as columns in the export.

Paid subscribers need a platform that can bill them. Kit, beehiiv Lite, Ghost(Pro) Publisher, Substack and Flodesk Everything can. MailCheap can't.

Time the change with your MailerLite billing in mind:

1. A downgrade takes effect at the end of your current billing cycle.
2. Switching from annual to monthly billing waits until the annual term ends.
3. Deleting subscribers doesn't lower this month's bill, because anyone active during the cycle still counts.

Keep your MailerLite account until at least one campaign has gone out from the new platform and replies to your sending address still reach you.

Choosing between MailerLite alternatives comes down to what you use today. For a broadcast-only newsletter, start with Kit Free or beehiiv Free. For a paid newsletter, compare Substack, beehiiv Lite and Ghost. For a large list without a paywall, compare MailCheap and Flodesk. And if you rely on MailerLite's automations and websites, compare Comfort with Power before you move.
