  <h2>Leave if you're paying for contacts you don't mail. Stay if Mailchimp runs your store.</h2>
  <p>Mailchimp’s marketing plans are Free, Essentials, Standard, and Premium. A contact is a person stored in your audiences. You pick a contact limit, and that limit sets the price and the send allowance. If you drift over the limit, Mailchimp adds a charge rather than stopping the month.</p>
  <p>Free includes up to 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, with a daily cap of 250. Essentials starts at $13 a month and Standard at $20 a month, both for up to 500 contacts. Essentials allows sends equal to 10 times the contact limit and stores up to 50,000 contacts. Standard allows 12 times the contact limit and supports larger lists. Check current pricing for your size on [Mailchimp’s pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/).</p>

## The alternatives

**Kit Free** is the strongest $0 option under 10,000 subscribers if you broadcast. Visual automations are paid. Details are on [Kit pricing](/pricing/convertkit/) and [Kit vs Mailchimp](/compare/convertkit-vs-mailchimp/).

**beehiiv Free** stops at 2,500 subscribers and includes unlimited sends and the recommendation network. Automations and paid subscriptions start on Lite. The ladder is [beehiiv pricing](/pricing/beehiiv/).

**MailerLite Comfort** starts at $12 at 500 subscribers and is $49 at the 5,000 tier. That can be the all-rounder if you want pages and automations. See [MailerLite vs Mailchimp](/compare/mailerlite-vs-mailchimp/).

**MailCheap** plans are a fixed price per email volume: Starter $19, Growth $39, Pro $79, on the allowances in the [review](/reviews/mailcheap/). Use it when the cadence fits and you do not need Mailchimp’s store journeys or A/B tests. There is no free hosted plan.

**Ghost(Pro)** is the alternative when the site and the members are the product, not the campaign. **Substack** is the alternative when you want $0 until readers pay.

## Moving the list

Export contacts. Archive or omit people you must not mail, and do not import them as active. Journeys, templates, and store connections do not move with a CSV. Rebuild the welcome email first. If Mailchimp also runs store automations you can't rebuild elsewhere, stay.

MailCheap’s import help lists Mailchimp among the CSVs it can read. Send a test to yourself before the list sees an issue.
