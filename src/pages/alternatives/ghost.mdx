  <h2>Keep Ghost if members log in. Leave it if you only needed the send.</h2>
  <p>Ghost(Pro) Publisher at a few thousand members is $55 to $105 a month and takes nothing from membership revenue. That is a fair price for a publication. It is a high price for a weekly email you could send on Kit for $0 or on MailCheap for $19 to $39. <strong>Switch to a sender only when the theme, the members, and the paywall are not the product.</strong> If they are, the alternatives are beehiiv and Substack, not a cheaper SMTP bill.</p>
</section>

Business is the team plan. At 1,000 through 10,000 members the monthly price in that same file is $239, and the yearly charge is $2,388, which is $199 a month. It climbs after 10,000: $319 a month at 25,000 members, $399 at 50,000, $479 at 100,000. You buy staff seats, higher limits, and priority support. Most solo newsletters never need it. If you are on Business to get around a Publisher limit you do not use, you are the reader this page is for.

Ghost counts members, free and paid, as people with an account on the site. Staff users are separate. Sends are not the meter. A daily letter does not push you into a higher band. A successful free signup form does. That is the surprise for people who came from a tool that only counted “subscribed and mailable.” A member who never opens the email still counts.

At 5,000 people, leaving Ghost for beehiiv Lite would **raise** the bill from $75 to $109 and add a network you may want. Leaving for Kit Free would drop it to $0 and delete the membership site. Leaving for MailCheap would drop a weekly send to $39 and delete the membership site. The table is not a ranking of better products. It is a map of what you are paying Ghost to be.

Substack is missing from the grid because its monthly fee is $0 and its tax on paid revenue is 10%. A Ghost publication doing $2,000 a month in memberships pays $75 to Ghost at the 5,000 band and would pay $200 to Substack, plus the loss of the theme. A Ghost publication doing $200 a month would pay $20 to Substack and might still miss the site. Revenue first, then the band. ## When Ghost is still the right invoice

Keep Ghost if readers have accounts, you use tiers, and the archive is a site people visit on purpose. The 0% fee is worth more every time membership revenue grows, while the band only steps when members do. Keep it if custom themes, multi-author workflows, or the portal are in use. Keep Starter if you are under 1,000 members, you do not sell anything, and $18 is acceptable for the site. Moving a Starter blog to a $19 email tool plus a separate host is two systems to save a dollar.

Keep it, also, if you are only angry at the latest band because the list grew. That is the product working. A 10,000-member publication at $105 a month is $0.0105 per member. You can find a lower number. You should ask what those members do on the site before you chase it.

## beehiiv, Substack, and Kit

beehiiv is the Ghost alternative for publishers who want recommendations and an ad network more than they want a theme. Lite is often more expensive than Publisher at the same headcount, and it includes tools Ghost does not have. The website inside beehiiv is real and it is not Ghost. If you have spent a year on a custom theme, budget a redesign, not an import. Pricing is in [beehiiv pricing](/pricing/beehiiv/). Fit is in [beehiiv alternatives](/alternatives/beehiiv/).

Substack is the Ghost alternative for writers who will trade the theme for distribution and accept 10% on paid. It is the wrong alternative if the reason you chose Ghost was independence from a platform’s network. You would be walking back into one.

Kit is the Ghost alternative for a creator who wants sequences and products and does not need the public archive to be a CMS. Free to 10,000 subscribers is the number that makes Ghost look heavy for a simple letter. Creator is how you get the automations Ghost’s member emails only partly resemble. See [Kit alternatives](/alternatives/convertkit/).

## MailCheap

MailCheap is the Ghost alternative we will recommend in one situation: the Ghost site is a thin wrapper around a send, you already have a home for the writing or you do not need one, and you do not sell memberships. Then Publisher’s price is a membership product you are not using. A weekly list of 10,000 is $39 on MailCheap Growth. A weekly list of 25,000 is $79 on Pro. You lose tiers, the theme, the member portal, and the 0% commerce Ghost was doing. You keep a newsletter, a basic site on the paid plan, segments, automations, and a lower invoice.

Do not recommend MailCheap to a Ghost publisher with paying members. There is nowhere for those members to go inside the product. Move them to beehiiv, Kit, MailerLite, or stay. The [review](/reviews/mailcheap/) is blunt about this on purpose. Our link may earn a commission. Ghost’s pricing page is not an affiliate relationship.

## MailerLite

MailerLite can host a site, sell a paid newsletter, and automate, at Comfort prices of $49 (5,000 tier), $89 (10,000), and $179 (25,000). That last figure is $10 more than Ghost Publisher at 25,000 members, so “cheaper than Ghost” is not automatic. At 5,000 and 10,000, Comfort undercuts Publisher and gives you a different site builder. Power is the unlimited-email step if you mail daily. It is a practical alternative for operators and a stylistic downgrade for publications that care about the theme. The plan details are in [MailerLite alternatives](/alternatives/mailerlite/).

## Self-hosting instead of switching

If the issue is Ghost(Pro)’s bill and the issue is not Ghost itself, self-hosting the open-source software is the alternative that keeps your theme and your members. You then provide the server, the database, upgrades, and the email delivery. Many self-hosters send through a separate provider and still pay per email there. Add that bill before you call self-hosting free. If you do not want to be on call when the upgrade fails, you want a hosted alternative, not a VPS.

MailCheap also offers an AGPL self-host of its own sender. That does not import a Ghost theme. It is a different app. Mentioning it here is only so you do not confuse “I can self-host Ghost” with “I can self-host anything.”

Export members before you cancel Ghost(Pro). Ghost’s admin can give you a CSV. Map paid status with care, and talk to Stripe if subscriptions must keep charging. A member export is a list of people, not a set of live Stripe subscriptions. If you move paid readers to beehiiv, Kit, or MailerLite, recreate the offer and charge a test card — yours — before you tell anyone the old portal is closed. MailCheap cannot take those subscriptions at all. Cancel the host after the new site resolves on your domain, not before. DNS is the migration. The CSV is the backup.

Give yourself one overlapping week. Keep Ghost’s domain responding, send one ordinary issue from the new platform to recent readers, and only then change the DNS. A clever redirect that loses the member login is a bigger failure than a boring week of two invoices. If the theme is the thing you will miss, you are not done deciding. Stay on Ghost, or self-host it, instead of discovering the loss after the export. The people who regret leaving are rarely the ones who only used Ghost as a send button. They are the ones who had a decade of posts, a custom theme, and a handful of loyal members, and who priced the invoice without pricing the archive.

If you are choosing a first tool rather than leaving Ghost, and you know you want members and a site, start on Ghost. The alternatives on this page are for the moment you discover you did not. The starting guide is [how to start a newsletter](/guides/how-to-start-a-newsletter/).
