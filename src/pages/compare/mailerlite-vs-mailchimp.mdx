## Cost as the list grows

MailerLite’s numbers are from [mailerlite.com/pricing.md](https://www.mailerlite.com/pricing.md), last updated on their file on 8 September 2026 and re-read on 24 November 2026. Annual billing is 10% less. On 16 June 2026, Growing Business was renamed Comfort and Advanced was renamed Power.

| Subscribers, up to | MailerLite Comfort | Comfort emails | MailerLite Power | Mailchimp |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 250 | Free is $0 if you also stay within 2,500 emails | 2,500 on Free | n/a | Free: 250 contacts, 500 sends, 250 a day |
| 500 | $12 | 5,000 | $25 | Essentials from $13, Standard from $20 |
| 1,000 | $19 | 10,000 | $39 | Check current pricing |
| 5,000 | $49 | 50,000 | $69 | Check current pricing |
| 10,000 | $89 | 100,000 | $129 | Check current pricing |
| 25,000 | $179 | 250,000 | $239 | Check current pricing |

A weekly letter to 5,000 people is about 21,700 emails. Comfort at that tier allows 50,000, so the weekly issue fits at $49. A daily letter to the same 5,000 people is about 151,700 emails. Comfort’s 50,000 cap does not fit. Power at that tier is $69 and removes the email cap.

Mailchimp Free stops at 250 contacts and 500 sends. Past that, open [Mailchimp’s pricing page](https://mailchimp.com/pricing/marketing/) and read the tier for your contact total, after you archive people you will never mail. This comparison does not rank Mailchimp on an estimated mid-tier price.

## What changes the choice

Both products send campaigns, run automations, and connect to stores. The differences that show up for a newsletter:

- **Who counts.** Mailchimp’s stored contacts, including unsubscribed people, move the tier. MailerLite’s active subscribers do. Cleaning a Mailchimp audience before you compare invoices is part of the job.
- **Email caps.** Comfort is a multiple of the tier. Power is the unlimited-email plan, up to 200,000 subscribers. Mailchimp Standard’s send limit is 12 times the contact limit you buy.
- **Paid newsletters.** MailerLite includes them on Comfort and Power, not on Free. Mailchimp is built more around campaigns and commerce journeys than around a reader paywall.
- **Free plan.** MailerLite Free is 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails after the June 2026 change. Mailchimp Free is 250 contacts and 500 sends. Neither is a home for a working newsletter of a few thousand people.
- **Stores.** If the Mailchimp account runs the shop, journeys and the store connection are the product. A newsletter tool will not recreate that. Stay.

## MailCheap, when the job is the send

If you do not need MailerLite’s sites, paid newsletters, or multivariate tests, and you do not need Mailchimp’s store journeys, MailCheap prices the emails instead of the stored contacts.

| Weekly list | MailCheap | MailerLite Comfort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 (about 4,300 emails) | $19 Starter | $19 |
| 4,000 (about 17,300 emails) | $19 Starter. Five issues are 20,000 emails, before welcomes. | $49 at the 5,000 tier |
| 10,000 (about 43,300 emails) | $39 Growth | $89 |
| 25,000 (about 108,300 emails) | $79 Pro | $179 |

MailCheap does not include paid newsletters, an ad network, a referral program, or A/B tests. It has no free hosted plan. For a plain weekly letter past Mailchimp’s free cap, it is often the lower paid invoice we can verify. For everything else MailerLite’s paid plans include, it is a smaller product.
