## Price comparison

The table uses each company’s month-to-month list price, checked on 24 November 2026. beehiiv’s tier table is the one published at [beehiiv.com/pricing.md](https://www.beehiiv.com/pricing.md), which beehiiv says is generated from the same plan data as the pricing page. MailCheap’s plan grid is the one on [mailcheap.ai/help/plans](https://mailcheap.ai/help/plans). Annual billing is cheaper on both sides: MailCheap yearly plans cost 10 months’ price, and beehiiv’s pricing page says yearly billing saves up to about 20% versus paying month to month.

Welcome emails and tests count toward MailCheap’s monthly email cap. beehiiv’s paid and free plans include unlimited sends, so a second issue in a busy week does not move you up a tier. That difference matters more than the headline price if you write more than once a week. The [cost calculator](/#calculator) applies the same 52 ÷ 12 email estimate so a month with five weeks is not a surprise.

<p class="method-note">MailCheap also caps subscribers by plan: Starter 10,000, Growth 40,000, Pro 100,000, Scale 250,000, Max 1,000,000, Ultra 2,500,000. A quiet list can hit the subscriber cap before the email cap. A daily list does the opposite. Both numbers have to fit.</p>

The 4,000-subscriber gap is $90 a month, or $1,080 over a year of monthly bills, before you count beehiiv’s annual discount. That is real money for a solo newsletter. It is also the price of a product that can charge readers, run ads, and trade recommendations with other newsletters. If those features earn more than the difference, beehiiv is the cheaper business even when MailCheap is the cheaper invoice. If they earn nothing because you will not use them, you are paying Lite for a send button.

Annual billing does not close the gap. beehiiv Lite at the 5,000-subscriber bucket is $1,140 a year, about $95 a month. MailCheap Starter on a yearly plan is 10 × $19 = $190 a year. The yearly comparison is still a different order of magnitude. We ignore short promotions on both sides. A temporary price is not a plan.

## What you give up on MailCheap

The lower bill comes with a shorter feature list. This is not a secret, and it should be the first thing you check — not the last. MailCheap’s own pricing page says every hosted plan includes the same features and that you are choosing a volume, not a feature tier. That is a different shape of product from beehiiv, where Free, Lite, Pro, and Enterprise change what you can do.

Read that table from the job, not from the logo. A writer who sells a paid tier inside the newsletter is not choosing between $19 and $109. They are choosing between a tool that cannot do the job and a tool that can. A writer who already sells sponsorships by email, and who only needs the broadcast to arrive, is in the opposite position: beehiiv’s ad network and boosts are optional, and the Lite invoice is optional too.

MailCheap counts subscribers as people who receive issues. Unconfirmed, dormant, unsubscribed, bounced, and complained contacts stay on the list and do not count toward the cap. beehiiv counts unique subscribed email addresses. The two definitions are close for a clean list and far apart for a messy one. If half your export is unsubscribed, MailCheap’s cap ignores them and beehiiv’s subscriber count should too, as long as you do not import them as active. Importing a dead list into either product is a deliverability problem before it is a pricing problem.

## When the cadence changes the winner

A weekly send hides MailCheap’s email cap. A daily send finds it.

At 4,000 subscribers, one issue a week is about 17,334 emails and fits Starter. Seven issues a week is about 121,334 emails. That is Pro, at $79 a month, still under beehiiv Lite at $109, but the gap has shrunk from $90 to $30 and you are now one busy month away from Scale at $149. beehiiv’s price does not move when you add a second send. If your editorial plan is “whenever there is news,” price the daily column in the [calculator](/#calculator), not the weekly one.

The same trap runs the other way for a small list. At 1,000 subscribers, beehiiv Free is $0 and includes unlimited sends, a website, a podcast within its download cap, and the recommendation network. MailCheap is $19 before you have written the first paid-for issue, and it still will not sell subscriptions. Recommending MailCheap under beehiiv’s free cap is a way to spend money for a shorter product. We do not do that.

There is a third case: you want Lite’s features and your list is still inside Free. You would pay $59 a month at 1,000 subscribers for automations, polls, and paid subscriptions, or $49 a month on annual billing. That is a feature purchase. MailCheap at $19 does not substitute for it. The honest comparison is Free versus staying put, or Lite versus another paid product that has the feature you came for. Kit’s free plan, covered in the [Kit alternatives](/alternatives/convertkit/) guide, is the other $0 option up to 10,000 subscribers if all you send is broadcasts.

## Moving a list from beehiiv

MailCheap documents a beehiiv import. In beehiiv, open Settings, then Export data, and export subscribers (full version) if you want custom fields, tags, and stats, not just email and status. beehiiv emails the file. In MailCheap, open Contacts, choose Import a CSV, check the column mapping, and confirm that the people in the file agreed to hear from you. Inactive, invalid, and unsubscribed readers are mapped so they do not get the next issue. Imports require a paid MailCheap plan. The steps live on [MailCheap’s beehiiv migration page](https://mailcheap.ai/migrate/beehiiv).

A few things the file will not move for you:

- Paid subscription state. MailCheap cannot bill those readers. If you have paying subscribers, this migration is the wrong project until you have another way to charge them.
- Recommendations, boosts, and the ad network. Those are beehiiv’s marketplace. They do not export.
- Automations. Rebuild only the ones you actually use. A welcome email is the one that matters on day one.
- Your beehiiv website, podcast, and community. Those stay on beehiiv unless you rebuild them somewhere else. Ghost is the usual answer when the site is the product. See [Ghost alternatives](/alternatives/ghost/) if you are choosing a publication host instead of a sender.

Send a test to yourself, then a plain issue to a segment of people who have opened recently, before you mail the whole file. MailCheap’s import can read open and click fields when the export contains them, and it says the first issue goes to the most engaged readers first. That is a sensible default. It is not a substitute for looking at the list. A bought or scraped list is against MailCheap’s import rules and will get an account blocked. Only move people who signed up.

If the migration is really about leaving subscriber pricing, read the longer [beehiiv alternatives](/alternatives/beehiiv/) roundup. Kit, Ghost, Substack, and MailerLite solve different pieces of what beehiiv bundles, and several of them are $0 at list sizes where MailCheap is already $19. The [beehiiv pricing](/pricing/beehiiv/) guide shows every published Lite and Pro bucket if you want the invoice you would be walking away from, in both monthly and annual form.
