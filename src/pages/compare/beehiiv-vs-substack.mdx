This is not a culture article. beehiiv and Substack both want to be the place a publication lives. They invoice that idea differently, they grow it through different recommendation systems, and they overlap enough that writers treat the choice as a personality. It is a spreadsheet plus one question: where do new readers come from?

We are not affiliated with either company. Nothing on this page is an affiliate link to them. MailCheap shows up only as a third option for people who discover, halfway through, that they do not want either product. Our financial relationship with MailCheap is disclosed on the [review](/reviews/mailcheap/) and does not apply to the beehiiv or Substack links here.

## The money, without a metaphor

Substack, from its help center: publishing is free at any list size. Paid subscriptions cost 10% of each payment, plus Stripe. The published US-card sketch is 2.9% + $0.30, and 0.7% for Stripe Billing on recurring charges. Custom domain: $50 once. Some countries do not pay the 10% yet. Read the article if you bill from one of them.

beehiiv, from its pricing file on 24 November 2026: Free is $0 up to 2,500 subscribers and does not include paid subscriptions. Lite at 1,000 subscribers is $59 a month or $49 on the annual effective rate. At the 5,000 bucket, which is what a 3,000 or 4,000-person list pays, Lite is $109 or about $95 annually. At 10,000, Lite is $139. At 25,000, $199. Pro is more, at every step, and you do not need Pro to get the 0% take rate. Lite already has it. Stripe still applies. beehiiv quotes 2.9% + $0.30.

Sends are unlimited on both. You will not get an overage for writing twice in a week. You will get a beehiiv tier change for adding subscribers, including free ones. Substack does not charge those free subscribers a platform fee. That asymmetry is the whole trap in “beehiiv takes 0%.” The 0% is on revenue. The tier is on headcount.

Annual beehiiv billing moves the crossover. Lite at the 5,000 bucket is about $95 a month if you prepay, so 10% overtakes it at about $950 of monthly subscription revenue rather than $1,090. Do not prepay a year to win a comparison you have not earned yet. Monthly Lite is the option to test the network. Annual is what you switch to after the publication is staying.

## What you are buying besides the fee

Both will host the writing, mail the list, and charge a card. The differences that change the work:

- **Recommendations.** Both have them. beehiiv includes the recommendation network on Free. Paid recommendations are a paid-plan feature. Substack’s recommendations live inside Substack’s reader app and its graph. They are not interchangeable. A subscriber acquired in one does not arrive with a note about the other.
- **Ads.** beehiiv has an ad network on paid plans, not in every region, and not as part of the trial. Substack is not that product. If you want the platform to sell ads against the letter, this comparison tilts to beehiiv even when the 10% math does not.
- **Automations and tests.** beehiiv puts automations and A/B tests on Lite and Pro, not on Free. Substack is a simpler editor and a simpler pipeline. If your publication is one post and a paywall, simple is a feature. If you run onboarding sequences, beehiiv is closer to Kit than Substack is.
- **Websites and extras.** beehiiv ships a website, a podcast with download caps, a community, and digital products, with limits by plan. Substack ships a publication page, notes, and the app. Ghost is the third name to add if “website” means a theme you control. It is not a Substack clone. See [Ghost alternatives](/alternatives/ghost/).
- **Custom domain.** beehiiv includes custom domains on paid plans, not on Free. Substack charges $50 once. Budget the domain either way if you think you might leave. Leaving a `substack.com` URL is a redirect problem. Leaving a domain you control is a DNS change.
- **Take rate.** 0% on beehiiv paid plans, 10% on Substack, Stripe on both. MailerLite and Ghost Publisher are the other 0% hosted options if you dislike both networks. [MailerLite alternatives](/alternatives/mailerlite/) has those prices.

Team seats, guest authors, and analytics history are beehiiv plan limits. A solo writer will not feel them. A small newsroom will, and will end up on Pro sooner than the fee table suggests. Pro at the 5,000 bucket is $199 a month. That is a $90 premium over Lite for branding removal, more seats, and the higher limits. It is not required to beat Substack’s 10%.

## Two networks, and how to measure them

Ignore slogans about which network is “bigger.” Open the subscriber source report you already have. If a quarter of last quarter’s new free subscribers came from Substack recommendations, that channel has a value. Price it as the subscribers times whatever a subscriber is worth to you, even if that worth is “they might pay later.” Then compare that value to the fee difference in the table. A network that delivers $300 a month of future paid revenue is not free, and it is not something you should delete to save $70 of platform fee.

If the report says almost everyone arrived from your own links, the network is a story you are telling yourself. In that case the fee and the tier are the whole decision, and you should stop reading essays about the culture of either app. Move or stay on the numbers. The same honesty applies to beehiiv boosts. A boost you do not run is not a benefit of staying, and a boost you have not tried is not a benefit of switching. Try it on the platform you are already on, if it exists there, before you migrate in order to maybe try it.

We did not run a growth experiment on either network for this page. Anyone who gives you a universal “you will gain 20% more subscribers” figure is inventing one. Your last ninety days are the dataset.

## Where writers get the crossover wrong

The usual mistake is to compare Substack’s 10% with beehiiv’s sticker at 1,000 subscribers, while sitting on a list of 12,000 free readers and $250 of paid revenue. beehiiv’s price for that list is the 25,000 bucket only if you have 12,000 — no, 12,000 is the 25,000 bucket, Lite at $199. Ten percent of $250 is $25. Switching would cost an extra $174 a month to save a fee you have inflated in your head. The list of free readers is what Lite prices. Substack ignores them.

The opposite mistake is a publication doing $5,000 a month on a list of 6,000, still paying 10% because “moving is a hassle.” The hassle is real. $500 a month against a $139 Lite bill is $361 a month, every month, after you are done moving. That pays for the hassle in a quarter. It does not pay for a dead recommendation channel. Look at the source report first, then at the quarter.

A third mistake is treating MailCheap or Kit as the winner of beehiiv versus Substack. They can be the right tool and they are a different question. Kit is free to 10,000 for broadcasts and can sell products. MailCheap is $19 and up and cannot sell subscriptions. If this page’s logic does not fit your publication, leave the page. Forcing a network tool to behave like a sender is how both invoices disappoint you.

## Who should pick which

Pick **Substack** if the list is mostly free, paid revenue is under the Lite bill, or the app is a measured source of readers you are not ready to replace. Pick it if you want the simplest possible paid switch and you accept the 10% as the cost of that simplicity. Pick it if you are starting this week and you do not yet know if anyone will pay. A monthly Lite subscription for 200 free readers is a costume.

Pick **beehiiv** if paid revenue clears the tier, or if you want the ad network and automations enough to pay Lite before the crossover. Pick it if you are under 2,500 and Free is enough, which makes the 10% a problem you do not have yet. Pick Pro only for a Pro reason: branding, seats, dynamic content, the higher ceiling. The tier table is [beehiiv pricing](/pricing/beehiiv/).

Pick **neither** if you want a membership site you theme yourself (Ghost), a creator CRM (Kit), or a cheap send with no marketplace (MailCheap, and only then). The migration notes for leaving either platform are in [Substack alternatives](/alternatives/substack/) and [beehiiv alternatives](/alternatives/beehiiv/). Export before you announce. Point the domain you control. Tell paying readers twice. Turn the old paywall off when the new one has charged a test card, including yours.

If you are still choosing a first platform, the order is reader, cadence, then tool. That order is [how to start a newsletter](/guides/how-to-start-a-newsletter/). beehiiv versus Substack is a later argument, and it is a better argument after you have a revenue number and a source report, not before.
