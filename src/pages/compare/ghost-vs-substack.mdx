## Price by scenario

Ghost’s monthly bands below are from the pricing file its site loads. The yearly page shows Starter at $15 a month ($180 a year) and Publisher at about $29 a month ($348 a year) for 1,000 members.

| Situation | Substack | Ghost(Pro) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 free readers, nothing for sale | $0 | $18 Starter. No paid memberships on this plan. |
| 1,000 members, $300 a month in subscriptions | $30, plus Stripe | $35 Publisher, plus Stripe, 0% platform fee |
| 5,000 members, $1,000 a month in subscriptions | $100 | $75 Publisher |
| 10,000 members, $2,000 a month in subscriptions | $200 | $105 Publisher |
| 25,000 members, $3,000 a month in subscriptions | $300 | $169 Publisher |

Substack does not charge for free readers. Ghost’s member count includes free and paid members. A large free audience raises Ghost’s tier and does not raise Substack’s 10%.

A custom domain on Substack is $50 once. Ghost(Pro) includes a free custom domain. Stripe applies on both paid setups. Substack’s help article quotes 2.9% + $0.30 for US cards and 0.7% for Stripe Billing.

## Site or network

Ghost is a membership website that also sends the newsletter. You get themes on Publisher, multiple tiers, and a 0% platform fee. You do not get Substack’s reader app or its recommendation graph. Ghost has its own recommendations and an Explore directory. We did not measure either network.

Substack is the faster way to charge readers and to be discovered inside Substack. You give up theme control and you pay 10% for as long as the paid tier runs. Leaving a substack.com address later is a redirect problem. Leaving a domain you control is a DNS change.

If you want beehiiv’s ad network in this decision, that is a third product. See [beehiiv vs Substack](/compare/beehiiv-vs-substack/).

## If you only need the email

MailCheap only sends email. Use it when the publication already lives somewhere else, or when you were paying Ghost(Pro) only to mail a list and you do not sell memberships.

A weekly list of 10,000 is about 43,300 emails, which is MailCheap Growth at $39. Publisher at 10,000 members is $105. The $39 plan does not include member login, themes, or paid subscriptions. If those are the product, the $105 is the relevant bill and MailCheap is the wrong comparison.
