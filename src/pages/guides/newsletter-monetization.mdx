<h2 id="how">How newsletter monetization works</h2>

Readers pay you, a sponsor pays you, or both. How to monetize a newsletter is the decision of which of those payments the software is allowed to touch. Paid subscriptions are one form of newsletter monetization. A sponsor line is another.

A paid subscription is the reader payment. You set the price. The platform either takes a percent, takes nothing and bills you a plan, or cannot do the sale. How newsletters make money in the subscription case is that gap between the price and the fees, times the number of people who keep paying. Churn is not in any vendor's pricing table. Do not treat a full list as a full set of payers.

Email monetization is the wider version: a course, a booking, or a file, not only the recurring letter. Kit's help article, updated September 3, 2026 and checked November 25, 2026, says commerce includes a paid newsletter on every plan, including Free. MailerLite's pricing page allows one digital product on Free and turns on paid newsletters only on Comfort and Power.

A sponsorship is a payment you usually invoice yourself. The letter is the ad. Any of the eight sending tools can carry that line, including MailCheap. The platform fee tables below do not apply to a sponsor check that never enters the platform. The commercial-email rules still apply to the issue. The setup page covers those rules: [how to start a newsletter](/guides/how-to-start-a-newsletter/).

An ad network is the platform finding the sponsor. beehiiv's pricing page lists the ad network on Lite and Pro, not on Free, not during the trial, and not in every region. The page does not publish a rate card. If the network is why you would pay for Lite, price Lite from the [beehiiv pricing](/pricing/beehiiv/) table and treat ad revenue as unknown until beehiiv shows it in the account.

<h2 id="make-money">How do newsletters make money?</h2>

Newsletter monetization, in practice, is reader payments, a sponsor you invoice yourself, or both. Reader payments are a subscription or a one-time digital product. Sponsor payments are a line in the issue. beehiiv's ad network, on Lite and Pro, is a third path, and the pricing page does not publish a rate for it. Starting a newsletter business means that mix plus the monthly software plan. The fee tables are the software's cut. They are not a forecast of what readers or sponsors will pay.

MailCheap has no percent to compute. The sending plan is the whole invoice, and the reader payment happens somewhere else. A 0% tool still has to be able to charge. MailCheap's plan cannot.

<h2 id="monetize">How to monetize a newsletter</h2>

To monetize a newsletter, match the fee to the payment you collect. You can charge for issues inside a tool that sells subscriptions, or monetize your email list through a checkout on another site. A few one-time product sales are a Kit or MailerLite commerce question until the product is a subscription. A $5 monthly price is a percent-versus-tier question, and the member count sets the tier. The [cost guide](/guides/email-marketing-cost/) prices the sending plan. The [platform guide](/guides/newsletter-platforms/) says which product fits the rest of what you need.

<h3 id="paid">How to start a paid newsletter on a plan that can charge</h3>

Start on a plan that lists the feature. Substack can charge on the free publishing plan, then keeps 10%. Kit can charge on Free, then keeps 3.5% plus $0.30 per USD sale. beehiiv can charge on Lite or Pro, not on Free. Ghost can charge on Publisher or Business, not on Starter. MailerLite can charge for a paid newsletter on Comfort or Power, not on Free. Flodesk can charge on Everything, not on Email or Free. MailCheap cannot charge.

Early on, many letters pair a free issue with one paid offer. Annual reader plans are the reader's prepay. They are separate from the annual software price.

A CSV of addresses does not recreate a paid subscription. Ghost bills in your Stripe account. <a href="https://help.kit.com/en/articles/4199324-sell-digital-products-with-kit-overview-and-faqs">Kit's commerce help</a>, updated September 3, 2026 and checked November 25, 2026, says Kit has you create a new Stripe account when you set up payments, even if you already have one.

<h2 id="profitable">Are newsletters profitable?</h2>

Some are. Profit is what remains after the platform fee, the monthly plan, Stripe, refunds, and the hours to write the issue. A fee at $10,000 a month of reader revenue is the platform's cut, and the rest of that $10,000 is not automatically profit. At $200 a month of Substack revenue, 10% is $20 and the publishing plan is $0. A $59 Lite plan costs more than that $20 until revenue grows, or until you need Lite for automations or the ad network. Count those lines for your list, then read the pair that matches the two tools you are choosing. [beehiiv vs Substack](/compare/beehiiv-vs-substack/) is the percent against the bucket. [Ghost vs Substack](/compare/ghost-vs-substack/) is the membership site against the percent. The switch pages, including [Substack alternatives](/alternatives/substack/), are for an account you already use.

<h3 id="outside">Sponsors, and checkouts that live somewhere else</h3>

You can monetize a newsletter the tool cannot bill. Put the sponsor in the issue, or link to a checkout you run elsewhere. MailCheap is that shape: a counted send and no reader paywall. Kit, MailerLite, and Flodesk can send the same kind of issue. Their own commerce is optional. Sponsorships are newsletter monetization the sending tool may never bill.

Price the sending plan on the [cost guide](/guides/email-marketing-cost/). If the other checkout also takes 10%, the fee still applies. The table above shows each tool's own cut when readers pay inside it.
