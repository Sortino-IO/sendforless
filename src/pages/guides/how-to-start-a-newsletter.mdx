## Decide who it's for

One sentence is enough: who it is for, and what they get that they would not get by browsing. “A Tuesday note on independent bookstore news for people who buy from shops, not chains” is specific. “Thoughts on books and life” is only a topic.

Put that sentence at the top of the signup page. If you cannot say what the next four issues are, wait a week and draft them before you pick software.

## Pick a cadence

Weekly is the schedule most people can keep next to a job. Daily is a different product: it multiplies the email count by about seven, and some plans that look cheap for a weekly letter run out of sends.

Write the day on a calendar for the next six weeks. If two of those weeks are already impossible, do not promise daily, and do not promise twice a week.

Example: a Sunday letter about one shop, one book, and one link. That is one send. A welcome email is a second send, once, to each new person. Tests are extra sends. Count them when you price MailCheap, because MailCheap counts every email it sends for you.

## Count the list you will actually have

Start from people who asked to hear from you: an existing site, a checkout, a talk, a form you already run.

Three useful planning numbers:

- **Now.** The people you can mail this month.
- **In six months.** A plain guess, such as “double, if the letter stays weekly.”
- **A long month.** Subscribers times the number of issues in a five-week month.

At 4,000 subscribers, one issue a week is about 17,300 emails in the 4.33-week estimate.

## Choose a platform for that send

Use the row that matches the letter, not a general ranking. Prices below are month-to-month USD list rates checked on 24 November 2026. Sources are on [How we test](/how-we-test/).

| Situation | Platform | What you pay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broadcasts only, under 10,000 subscribers | Kit Free | $0. Sequences and visual automations need Creator, which is $39 a month up to 1,000 subscribers. |
| A site and recommendations, under 2,500 subscribers | beehiiv Free | $0. Paid subscriptions, ads, and automations start on Lite. |
| You will charge readers on the platform, and revenue is still small | Substack | $0 to publish. 10% of subscription revenue, plus Stripe, when readers pay. |
| You send on a schedule, you are past those free caps, and you sell elsewhere | MailCheap | Starter is $19 a month for 20,000 emails and 10,000 subscribers. Growth is $39. |
| You want the price to stay flat as the list grows | Flodesk Email | $46 a month, or $38 a month billed annually. Unlimited subscribers and sends. The free plan does not send. |
| Members log in to a site | Ghost(Pro) | Starter is $18 a month up to 1,000 members and cannot sell memberships. Publisher starts at $35. |

MailCheap is the lower paid bill for a weekly letter that fits Starter or Growth and does not need a paywall, an ad network, a referral program, or A/B tests. At 4,000 subscribers and one issue a week, Starter’s $19 covers the 4.33-week estimate. A month with five weekly sends uses all 20,000 emails. If you also send welcome emails, price Growth at $39 before you switch.

If Kit Free or beehiiv Free already sends the issue, stay there. A $19 plan is an extra bill, not a requirement.

## Set up the first send

1. Buy a domain you control if you expect to leave the first platform. Substack charges $50 once for a custom domain. beehiiv includes custom domains on paid plans, not on Free. MailCheap includes a custom domain on paid plans.
2. Turn on the welcome email before you announce the letter. On a free Kit plan you can broadcast, and sequences need Creator. A single welcome issue can be a broadcast you send by hand for the first month.
3. Mail yourself, then ten people who will tell you if the links break.
4. Publish the signup form in one place you already have an audience. Do not open five networks on day one.
5. Send the first real issue on the day you named.

Import only people who asked for the letter. A purchased list is a deliverability problem on every platform in this guide, and MailCheap’s import rules say it can get an account blocked.

## When the bill changes

Come back when one of these happens:

- You cross 2,500 subscribers and beehiiv Free has ended. Lite at the up-to-5,000 bucket is $109 a month. [beehiiv vs Substack](/compare/beehiiv-vs-substack/) is the page if readers are paying you.
- You cross 10,000 subscribers and Kit Free stops sending. The Creator price for that list size is on [Kit’s pricing page](https://kit.com/pricing). For a weekly broadcast, MailCheap Growth is $39 at 10,000 subscribers (about 43,300 emails). See the [Kit alternatives](/alternatives/convertkit/).
- You turn on paid subscriptions. Substack’s 10% becomes a real invoice. Compare it with beehiiv Lite, Ghost Publisher, and MailerLite Comfort before you assume a monthly plan is cheaper.
- A five-week month plus welcome emails no longer fits the MailCheap plan you bought. Move up one plan, or send less.
