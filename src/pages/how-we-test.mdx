## What a comparison is

sendforless answers a narrow pair of questions. For a given list size and a given number of issues per week, what monthly list price does each platform’s public pricing imply? Which documented features make that price the wrong comparison?

That is a reading of the pricing page and the help center, not a test of the sending infrastructure. Worked examples are things like a 4,000-person weekly letter, a daily letter that trips an email cap, and an import path you can follow from the vendor’s own steps.

The date on each guide is the date of that reading: 24 November 2026. If a number here and a number on a vendor page disagree, pay the vendor page. Write to [hello@sendforless.com](mailto:hello@sendforless.com) and name the URL.

Where a list size is not in that material, the page says **check current pricing** and links the vendor. We do not rank a tool on a number we did not read. Kit’s price above the stops above, and Mailchimp’s price above 500 contacts, are in that group.

Do not interpolate between published buckets. On beehiiv, 4,000 subscribers pays the up-to-5,000 price.

## Counting a month of email

The planning estimate is subscribers × issues per week × 4.33, rounded up. The 4.33 is 52 weeks ÷ 12 months. It keeps a weekly newsletter from being priced as if every month had four sends.

A 4,000-person weekly letter is about 17,334 emails on that estimate. A month with five issues to the same 4,000 people is 20,000 emails, before welcome emails and tests. MailCheap Starter includes 20,000 emails. The average month fits. The long month uses the whole allowance before those extra messages. If you also send welcomes or tests, price the next plan.

We then pick the cheapest published plan where both the email allowance and the subscriber allowance fit. Welcome mail, tests, and automation mail are not added on top of the issue estimate. On MailCheap they count. The row says so. It does not guess your welcome volume.

Payment processing, taxes, and add-on seats are excluded. So are promotions. Annual prices are labeled as annual, and the “per month” figure is the yearly charge divided by 12 when the vendor publishes it that way.

## When MailCheap is named

MailCheap is named when all of these are true:

- A free plan does not already cover the send. Under 10,000 subscribers, Kit Free often does. Under 2,500, beehiiv Free often does. Substack has no monthly fee until readers pay.
- The cadence fits a published MailCheap plan at a price below the other paid tools that also fit.
- You do not need paid subscriptions, an ad network, a referral program, or A/B tests inside the sending tool.

If one of those fails, the page says which other product fits. A lower price is not a recommendation by itself. Ghost can be the right higher bill when the site is the product. beehiiv can be the right higher bill when you use the ad network or paid subscriptions. Staying on Mailchimp can be the right higher bill when the account runs a store.

The calculator sorts known prices and labels the MailCheap row as Affiliate. “Lowest paid price” appears only when MailCheap is the lowest known paid row in that scenario. A $0 plan can still be the one that fits.

## What we do not measure

Inbox placement. Deliverability over time. Support at 11 p.m. Whether a recommendation network will work for your topic. Private enterprise quotes. Prices in currencies other than the vendor’s published USD list. The quality of an editor, beyond the limits the vendor documents.

We will describe a documented limit. We will not describe a product test we did not run.
